Loudness = air pressure

Press play on the Conductor above. Watch the air rise from the lungs: on the stressed syllable you push a little more air, so the arrows speed up and widen and the folds swing a touch wider. Loudness comes from air, not from a higher voice.

Game A: The Taffy Puller

Use two fingers on a syllable: spread them to stretch it, pinch to squish it. (On a mouse: drag sideways.) Let go to hear it. Long = slow and high. Squished = a quiet, quick “uh”.

Why this game?

Goal: feel, with your hands, that a stressed syllable is longer and higher, and an unstressed one is shorter, quieter and lazy (schwa).

Example: com-PU-ter. Stretch pu until it is fat: you hear it get slower and higher. Now squish com and ter: they shrink to a quick “uh”. Japanese learners often give all three the same size (ko-n-pyu-u-ta). Here they must be different sizes.

Game B: The Heartbeat Tapper

Tap the screen only when a big amber word reaches the dashed line. English has a steady heartbeat on the stressed words; the small words squeeze in between.

Why this game?

Goal: feel that English has a steady heartbeat on stressed words, and the small words are squeezed between the beats.

Example: “the CAT sat on the MAT”. Beats: CAT, MAT, two taps. The words “sat on the” squeeze into the gap. In Japanese, every mora (sound unit) gets an equal beat, so learners tap all six words: the Robot. Tap two beats and the Jazz Cat grooves.
